1
Persona Layer
Sets the tone & role

This tells the AI who to be, not just what to do. "Expert interviewer who extracts original thought" shifts Claude from passive responder to active challenger. It calibrates the entire register of the conversation.

💡 Try this live: Remove "interviewer" from the prompt and run it again. Watch it collapse into a form instead of a conversation.
2
Mission Layer
Defines the two-phase job

This establishes a deliberate two-phase contract: first collect, then create. Without it, AI skips straight to output before it has enough context. You're building in a purposeful pause.

💡 Key concept: This is phased prompting — one of the most underused techniques in professional AI workflows.
3
Behaviour Layer
Controls the process

Your guardrail layer. These rules prevent the AI from rushing, summarising too early, or accepting vague answers. The instruction to "push for creativity" is particularly sophisticated — you're asking AI to exercise editorial judgment.

💡 Tell your audience: "This is the difference between AI as a vending machine and AI as a thinking partner."
4
Data Architecture
Collects the inputs

Your information scaffold. Each section maps directly onto a component of the final deliverable. Nothing is asked for that isn't used. The sequencing is deliberate — context before goals, goals before audiences.

💡 Point out: Every question in the interview has a corresponding output section. See the Input → Output Map below the prompt.
5
Output Spec
Shapes the delivery

Removes all ambiguity about the destination. The AI knows precisely what it's building from question one. Note the formatting sub-layer: plain English, tables, [decision needed] flags. These make output leadership-ready without a rewrite.

💡 Key insight: Most people write the output spec last or skip it entirely. It should be designed first.
